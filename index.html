    A world in conversation, read by an oracle.

    The Present Tense gathers hundreds of headlines from publishers in dozens of countries and lays them out as a field: softly geographic, slowly drifting, every one of them a door to the original report. At the centre of the field is an oracle. It reads the collection and proposes constellations: possible narratives that join a headline in Lagos to one in Taipei to one in Córdoba.

    This is a Glass Bead Game played with the news. The readings are interpretations, not findings. Each one commits to a claim, shows its evidence, and then argues against itself in a final rung called Break this reading. Read both halves. The point is the argument, not the verdict.

    What you are looking at

    Headlines come from the GDELT Article List, which indexes world news metadata: titles, links, publishers, languages and timestamps. Nothing else is copied. Each headline links to its publisher. The map places a headline at its publisher's country, never at the event's location. Headlines appear in English by default; the original wording is always one tap away, and a country's press is not that country's people.

    Geography on this page is loose on purpose. Headlines are pushed apart so you can read them, so Europe swells and the oceans fill. Hollow rings mark countries we have publishers for but heard nothing from in this collection. That is a gap in our listening, not a silence in the country. Many countries have no publisher here at all. The field is partial and says so.

    The tide

    Every edition is compared with the one before. Headlines that arrived, stayed or departed are marked, and departed headlines can be shown as ghosts. The present is only legible against the recent past.

    How it stays live

    A scheduled job collects a fresh bounded sample of headlines, translates new ones, and asks Claude to compose a new edition of readings against a strict schema: a claim, sourced fragments, and a mandatory case against. The site refuses an edition whose citations do not resolve. Collection without a key keeps the previous readings and marks them as older than the headlines.
